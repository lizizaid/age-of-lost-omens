For [[Barton]], this might have been another ordinary morning. He might have woken up in his bed in his parents' small apartment. They might not have much, but what they have is more than enough. He would sit with them for a warm breakfast and fine conversation before taking a cart up into the [[Korvosa#The Heights|Heights]], to another day of studies at [[Korvosa#The Acadamae|The Acadamae]]. It's not an easy trail, and he is only at his second year of study, but between the casting of cantrips he can see approving nods of his instructor - he is on his way to becoming a star student, if he keeps on working hard. Later that evening, he might go with his close friends to one of the uptown pubs and have a hearty conversation about politics and magical theory and everything in-between. And later one of his friends of noble upbringing might even sneak them into an upper-class ball, where Barton would dance with the beautiful daughters of nobles to the magnificent sounds of a playing band. And as he would return to his house, and lay down in his warm bed, he would smile as he feels the satisfaction of a day well spent, and a life well lived, and he would close his eyes.  

And as he opened his eyes, he found this dream quickly shattered, as a stream of dirty wash water fell down on his face.  

The cold water quickly awoke Barton, who found himself thrown in some dirty alley in [[Korvosa#Old Korvosa|Old Korvosa]]. Looking up, it seemed as though a woman in the window above him has poured her used wash water from her bucket down, not even noticing the homeless man sleeping under her apartment. After soaking most of the water from his clothes and cursing his fate, Barton got up and stretched.  It was only starting to dawn and so the city was just now waking from it's stupor; The half-rotten half-crumbling buildings of Old Korvosa and her invigorating smells of wet trash have assaulted his senses, just as have the itch from his unkempt beard, but which did not itch enough for him to do something about it. Instinctively reaching for his trusty weapon, Barton felt a frightful chill as he suddenly realized it was not there. Looking around, he quickly spotted the thing - wrapped around the arms of yet another homeless sleeping in the same alley as he was, an old dwarf man with wild white hair. It would seem the bastard have stolen the weapon at night, but in his drunken state could not get far and fell asleep just a few meters away from Barton; He, in turn, quietly tried to grab the weapon away from the snoring dwarf, but in the process awoke the thief. "H... hey! Give that back! That's mine!" screamed the alerted drunkard, who suddenly froze in terror when Barton aimed the gun at his face, finger on the trigger. "Do not move. You know who this truly belongs to." he said angrily. "P...please! It's just that.... the metal was still warm last night! I just wanted something warm to hold on to in my sleep!" cried the pathetic dwarf. "Well, if you keep talking, I will put such a hole in you that you'll feel warm forever on." Barton replied coldly. "Now get out of here and never return, you wrench." Crying in fear once more, the dwarf took his legs and got out of that place, as Barton sighed and returned the gun, carved with magical runes, to it's handle on his hip. He opened a crumpled note from his pocket - a list he made of possible shops he could hit for parts - and realized it was getting late, and if he wished to get there before they open for business he must be quick.  

Making his way through the streets of Old Korvosa, he witnessed the views of the district which never failed to appear grim and depressing. The old ramshackle building that always seem on the verge of collapse, the poor and downtrodden who plod along from their houses to their daily jobs, the street urchins playing with sorry replicas of balls made of rotting cabbages wrapped in empty flour sacks, and of course, the rows of beggars setting up their shops for the day. Passing by a trash heap where some home owner nearby throws his copy of the Korvosan Post first thing in the morning, Barton stopped to grab [[1) Korvosan Post - 12th Desnus|today's copy]]. Walking and reading, it seems today's big story revolved around the king's mysterious ailment, and people claiming the Curse of the Crimson Throne have finally come to take old [[Eodred Arabasti II|Eodred]] away. Though intriguing, such things could not be of less importance for Barton at his current state. He had to stay focused on his goals. So pressing forward, and keeping a somewhat paranoid eye for any suspicious looking individuals, he quickly walked over the bridges and into [[Korvosa#Midland|Midland]], to find his unfortunate victim for today...

For [[Kebub Urag]], his waking dream was less of a dream, more of a vision. A mystical mirage he could not explain, not with words known to his people. For in those images that haunted him since that fateful night when he died, but did not really die, he saw death itself. He saw a river of souls, of ancestors and animals and people he did not know, who pulled him gently down the river and into an endless sea of black nothingness, where the only thing gazing back at him from the abyss was a great white skull, who looked at him with empty eyes, before too sinking into the dark nothing, at which point Kebub woke up from the vision.  

He woke up in his furs in his tent, with the smell of wet leather from the morning dew and the hubhub of his people getting ready for the day outside. He could not explain what he saw, not better then he could explain what he saw at any particular night since that night he died. He could only feel the strain of his battle hardened muscles and the phantom pain of his missing eye. And so, with no better option, he started his day as well.  

He joined the rest of the [[Sokan-Quah|clan]] in the communal feasting tent, where for breakfast, they had large chunks of smoked auroch meat. He looked at them eating and laughing, a stranger among his own people, a guest at his own family's household, knowing that his green skin would forever separate him from his clanspeople. It was then when the Quah's revered shaman, old blind [[Thousand Bones]], signaled him to come dine next to him. The shaman's grandson, and one of Kebub's only true friends, [[Gaekhen]], was also there. "How do you fare, old friend?" asked the kindly shaman. "I know it has not been easy for you since your... incident." "I am not sure I can explain what is going on with me..." murmured Kebub, "I see terrible visions every night, and I feel like my mind is elsewhere from here." "Indeed, our people have known Death for ages now, but your connection with it is... special." Thousand Bone remarked sadly. "After all, you have had first-hand experience with it. It will take time to heal from what has happened to you. I do not know if it gives you solace, old friend, but since death is the endpoint of all life, it just has to mean that life is worth living!" he tried to cheer the grim orc, but failing to do so, continued with a request. "I want to ask a favor of you. Me and my grandson will go into the city today to talk with one of their leaders, to try and secure job opportunities for our people to earn their living. I want you to come with us to share of your experience working in the city, to show we are honorable and hard-working. I'm sure your views will be well appreciated." And though Kebub doubted that very much, he had nothing but respect for his shaman and his ideas, and so agreed to come with.  

The three packed their bags and walked down from the hill where their encampment stood, and crossed the great stone bridge over the river and into Korvosa. Passing merchants setting up their stalls and people coming and going, they finally reached the city's gate, where a bored guard draped in a red tabard took one good look at the eccentric Shoanti group before asking, "Papers, please." Though Thousand Bones protested, saying that their arrival time has been agreed upon with the [[Korvosa#Arbiters|Arbiters]] and that, in fact, the very same guard has let him in the last time he visited, the guard only rolled his eyes, and angrily asked once more for papers. "No entrance without papers." He shrugged. It only took some very polite words and a strong push on the shoulder from the massive Kebub for the guard to re-think his requirements. He suddenly remembered Thousand Bones and his meeting and let the group into the city, still shaking somewhat. Gaekhen angrily protested that everyone in this wretched city treated them the same, that this guard's behavior was the proof,  but Thousand Bones hushed his rebellious grandson, asking him to show patience for the sake of the clan, and Kebub tended to agree - no good can come of fighting these other people, that he knew for certain. And so the three of them, strangers in an alien city full of alien noises and smells, made their way to their destination...

[[Zim]] knew that what he saw was not a dream and not a nightmare, nor was it a vision. For Zim knew he saw the future. And the future was bleak.  

What he saw in front of him was the city in flames. The buildings crumbled and ruined and burning in a great inferno. The broken streets filled with the bodies of the dead. The screams of the few who survived echoed from afar, as they brutally killed each other over the last pieces of food and water, trying in vain to delay the inevitable for one day more. The blood from the heaps of dead has coalesced into a terrible read river, spilling into a boiling red sea, and above it all stood a flaming crimson sun, patiently burning the pitiful ruins of the city away with it's scorching rays. The end has reached Korvosa, and all Zim could do was stand there and watch the last miserable breaths of his once home, knowing for sure that this is to be his future, and that nothing could ever be done to stop it.  

He woke up with a scream in a puddle of sweat in his bed. From the open window, the cool sea breeze blew and rays of morning sun, not crimson but yellow and warm, shone in his eyes. His current resident was at the [[Korvosa#1. The Soldado house|house of the Soldado family]], whom have given him refuge since he was abandoned by his traveling circus family. Outside of his small room, the chaotic noises of the Soldado children wreaking havoc have already started early. Stepping into the living room, he found the family's matron, [[Tayce Soldado|Tayce]], attempting to instill order in her children while cooking breakfast at the same time. "Zim, my dear, you are awake, good. Would you be so kind as to help me a little bit?" she winced as her youngest daughter, Brienna, rushed pass a vase and almost knocked it down. Zim, still only half-awake from his nightmarish future reality, could only mutter something in return about a city burning and the end of the world. "Well, as you can see, beloved, I am kind of in a middle of the world ending around me as we speak", she crassly returned, pointing out to the discord around the house, as she was already accustomed to Zim's spewing unnerving and cryptic riddles, and learned to gently ignore his ramblings. "So please, be a dear and go but me some eggs so I could finish the meal!" handing him some coins she sent the dreamy youth on his way. On his way to the door, Zim could not help but to comfort the poor children who did now know what was soon coming for them, and they, in turn, were just happy to get some affection from the weird cousin who have somewhat become a part of their family these last few months.  

Soon, Zim found himself crossing the streets of [[Korvosa#Trail's End|Trail's End]], watching his fellow [[Varisia#Varisians|Varisians]] start their day; He too was a stranger among his own community, though he felt the people of the neighborhood grew to accept him as one of their own very quickly. Standing in line to buy eggs in the local food market, Zim took interest in the burly man standing before him in line - more specifically, in the copy of the Korvosan Post he was reading. "Did you hear that, my boy? It seems good old Eodred is not long for this world." The man said to Zim, "Well, good riddance to that devil, I say! Truly a cause for celebration. Perhaps we should strike out the band tonight at the tavern, and you could play us one of your card tricks..." Zim, however, was more interested in a small notice showing at the middle of the paper, claiming that the young queen has apparently lost her beloved brooch while sending it for repairs, and that she would handsomely reward anyone who might find and return the bejeweled bauble. Easy gold, if he could only divine where the trinket is hidden.  

At any rate, he returned to Tayce with her eggs, and though she thanked him, she had one more favor to ask. "Don't be mad, now," she said apologetically, "but if you plan on continuing to live in this house, you will have to partake in taking care of the household expanses. You should go out into the city today and find a job." "That seems... rather reasonable." Zim agreed begrudgingly. "Just find an honest job!" Tayce warned him as he left the house once more. "Our people suffer from prejudicial biases enough even without you inflating them with your scams and tricks, my dear!"   

Before even leaving the street the house resides in, Zim immediately found himself face to face with the curly hair and mischievous smile of [[Rita Bevenari|Rita]], greeting him hello. "Hiya there, Zim! Have I heard correctly? Is Tayce gonna kick you out of the house if you don't start to make some money for her?" Before Zim had the opportunity to protest her espionage and nose-sticking, she had quieted him with her suggestion for a job opportunity. "You should try to find Devargo at the last pier of the old docks if you want some easy money." "Who is he?" asked Zim, who have not heard of the man. "He is one of us. A good man, and he will hook you up with a job, for sure." promised Rita. That was all of the convincing Zim needed - leaving the cheerful girl behind, he started his way to Old Korvosa, to meet this Devargo and hopefully, for some time, try to forget about the haunting imagery he felt would soon come to pass...

It was almost opening hour when Barton, sweating and out of breath, finally made it to the streets of Midland's commercial area. People were already out and about, and he knew he does not have much time. He spotted his target - an eccentric gear shop by the name of [[Korvosa#1. Gadgets and Gizmos|Gadgets and Gizmos]] that usually sells machinery parts to Korvosa's emerging factories. Taking a quick stroll, he found a back door leading to the shop. The door was locked, and Barton's attempt to pick it using his set of old lockpicks proved futile. With the hubhub of the trade day increasing and the pressure growing, Barton took a big breath, and taking a big risk, swung with his shoulder and knocked the old wooden door on it's axis, making a lot of noise in the process. He quickly stumbled into the dusty storeroom beyond, finding his required treasure - bits of gear and metallic bobs, needed for maintaining his gun and for the creation of bullets. He was taking as much as he could in his bag, and in the process of filling a sack of black powder from a barrel, when he heard a squeaky voice coming from the other side of the building - "Who are you, intruder? I can hear you! Stop right there!" With no time to think, Barton ran to the fallen door, raising it back and holding it, just as the opposite door from the shop to the storeroom burst open, a [[Gnome|gnomish]] figure with messy red hair and stained with oil appearing at the threshold, holding some kind of a crossbow-adjacent contraption with shaking hands. "Who are you? What... what do you want from me?!" he squealed, pointing the thing at Barton. "Please, I meant no harm, sir. My name is... Bob." Barton replied. "I happened to walk through this alley when, leaning too much, I accidentally knocked down this door and stumbled into your shop..." understanding his lie is lousy at best, Barton also attempted a manner of intimidation, moving his coat slightly to showcase his gun. "And you just happened to lean on this random door?!" yelled the gnome, becoming red. "Are you sure that you haven't... hold up. Is that... a gun? A real gun, here in Korvosa?!" his tone immediately changed from annoyed to excited, and he started bombarding "Bob" with questions about his weapon. Barton, confused, took the opportunity to quietly hide the sack of black powder in his bags while explaining that, indeed, he crafted the gun by himself - much to the gnome's delight. He introduced himself as [[Barleton Fink]], a tinkerer and a private admirer of firearms. Sensing an opportunity arising from his misfortune, Barton indulged the man's curiosity, and - feeling his stomach rumble after not eating much these last few days - even suggested Barleton buy him lunch and they'll continue their discussion. The gnome was about to agree when the shop's bell signaled the first customers for the day, and, apologizing, Barleton rushed back to the counter. "Do come back after work hours, though, my friend! I'd love to have dinner together, at least!".   

Still hungry, but at least not caught and with all of the necessary components, Barton made his way through the crowd into some quiet alley in Midland, and went up some stairs to an old ruined apartment he knew was abandoned for years now - once of dozens of such houses in the city, left vacant almost a century before when people deserted en masse to form the city of [[Magnimar]] to the north, and were never managed to be re-populated by the government. Settling down the stolen gears on his coat, he got to work for a few hours - constructing new ammo for his gun. As the noon sun struck from the broken windows, he took a break, looking at himself through a piece of broken glass. He sighed at the remainder of the man he became - a walking, broken mess. He took some time to trim down his overgrown beard, managing a sad smile through tired eyes.  

Not being to ignore his hunger any longer, Barton left the apartment and made his way to Midland's fish markets, buying some simple dumplings filled with leftover fish innards - barely eatable, but also the cheapest meal available. He sat down near some fountain in [[Korvosa#2. Eodred's Square|Eodred's Square]] and forcefully slurped down his food, when suddenly he became tense, feeling as someone is watching him intently. His pressure turned to confusion when he noticed the culprit - a young woman in bright clothing, who seems to take good looks at him from across the plaza and then back at her painting canvas, drawing with focus. Barton decided to approach the curious street artist, who blushed when he approached. "Well, I guess you noticed me painting you, haven't you? I like to draw people I find striking. Here, take a look. It's still a work in progress, of course...." she showed him the draft, and there he saw himself; Not an especially flattering or heroic portrait, per se, but rather a very observant and personal one, capturing his dirtiness and tiredness in the same manner as the unshattering resolve in his eyes. The beautiful, touching picture stirred something in Barton, who blushed himself. "I don't find myself very striking. I'm just another homeless man, living on the streets." "Well, I find that the most interesting people to paint are usually the ones we tend to ignore as we cross the street everyday." She offered a smile back. "What's your name?" He introduced himself again as Bob, a very common name in the empire of Cheliax, and told her he's an engineer by trade. "I'm [[Trinia Sabor|Trinia]]," the woman said, "I paint people on the street for my living. Although, I recently managed to score a bigger gig..." she looked to both sides before adding, quietly: "They hired me to paint a portrait of King Eodred himself! Wanna see?". She flipped a page on her canvas and revealed the portrait, which captured the king's deep essence in very much the same manner as Barton's did; This time, revealing a man who was sick, confused, and very much unwell.

"Just like the rumors say, good old Eodred is in a very bad state." Trinia whispered. "He seemed out of it for most of the painting session, mumbling incoherently. I'm not sure why the crown wanted me to paint him at this state... I'm not even sure why they decided to hire me off the streets!" She gave a light chuckle, "I guess some official took a liking to my skills. Well, if our darling grandpa will not get better soon, I fear Korvosa will have a new queen on the Throne - before we know it, she will spend all of the treasury on dresses and perfume! Well, nevermind such dreary thoughts. I would very much like to hear about your work as well!" Their conversation moved from politics to their respective professions (where an offshoot comment about how Bob should utilizie his skills and attempt to apply to the [[Korvosa#The Acadamae|The Acadamae]] struck a small blow to Barton's soul) and even a friendly philosophical debate on the nature of art, and whether it could be found in such a mechanical work such as Barton's. Though he took a liking to the woman, Barton's tried his luck once more and asked her to buy them some lunch that does not involve fish guts. This time, however, Trinia happily agreed ("after all, I need to spend the small fortune I got from the Eodred portrait somehow!") and went off to the markets. Barton took the opportunity to freshen up, washing his face in the fountain. While looking at his reflection in the water, Barton noticed something curious - a Harrow card floating in the fountain toward him. He picked up the card, revealing *The Owl*, a large nocturnal predator with sharp eyes painted on it. On the card's back he found writing:  

*"I know what the Lamm family has done to you. I know you crave revenge.  
They have wronged me as well. I too want to see justice done.  
If you wish to achieve your heart's desire, come to my home at 3 Lancet Street on sunset."*  

Pocketing the weird card, Barton took some time to wonder at it's meaning, but soon Trinia came back with two meals of fried fish with potatoes, and the two happily continued their conversation for an hour or two more. As the sun started to set, Barton excused himself and departed, walking to the address written on the mysterious card. He kept wandering what this night had in store for him...

Through the gazes of weary crowds and suspicious guards alike, the three Shoanti men made their way northwards until finally they arrived before their destination: Longacre Building, a dreary, long cube of dark stone surrounded by sharp metallic fences. Entering it's gothic halls, they passed alert guards, and people wearing dark suits - the Arbiters of Korvosa, hurrying along to one meeting or another, paying no heed to the strange group. From time to time, a scream or a defeated sigh echoed from below them - no doubt the sounds of prisoners held in the dungeons beneath Longacre. "What awful treatment of criminals. The merciful act would have been to execute them - send them to their ancestors to atone." remarked Gaekhen, which only prompted a low grunt from Kebub in return.  

Some guards finally brought them into an office; A bleak room missing any decoration apart some very basic furniture. Near a desk sat an old, shriveled woman with a hawkish look, finishing the process of signing some papers. Her very presence radiated contempt.  "Senior Arbiter [[Zenobia Zenderholm]]," started Thousands Bones with a courteous bow, "I thank you for taking the time to meet us today. I hope this meeting shall prove a stepping stone in the relations between my Quah and the people of this fine city. As you recall, I have inquired about providing work opportunities for-" he was abruptly stopped by the Arbiter. "Yes, Mr Thousand Bones, I clearly remember your inquiry." She said with a raspy voice, which has no doubt been used in hundreds of court debates. "I have exactly 10 minutes until my next appointment, so i'll make this quick. I have considered your offer, and decided to refuse work permits for your people in our streets. However, the noble caste of Korvosa has grumbling over the shortage of available retainers in the last few years. Thus, I do offer your people job opportunities - as household staff, servants and butlers for our noble houses." "This is preposterous!" Thousand Bones' white eyes grew large, "Our people are strong of both arm and mind, capable of many honorable professions, yet you expect us to act as simple maid-slaves to your pompous rich?! By my ancestors, mistress Zenderholm, I beg you reconsider! Here, I have brought with me today Kebub Urag, one of my finest men, to share his experiences as a worker at your docks, and-" once more, he was shut off by the Arbiter's hand, who turned to look at Kebub, straight in the eyes. Hers tried to appear inquisitive but seemed more demeaning; His tried to appear rageful, yet were mainly tired. "An orc Shoanti - a curious combination. A dangerous combination." She said slowly. "I have heard what orcish clans do when they attack settlements on the frontiers. What devastation they bring. And throughout the years I have received no shortage of reports of brutal crimes made by orcs in this very city. Tell me, Mr. Urag. Have you never felt that ancestral rage burning up within you while you worked at my city? Have you never wished to unleash that violence on some poor sod on my streets? To capture some man or woman, and do vile deeds to them, take your revenge upon them for all the prejudice you must have felt throughout your years?" This, again, prompted no response but a low, angry growl from Kebub. With a smirk of satisfaction, Zenobia turned her gaze towards Thousand Bones once more. "Allow me to explain the reason for my refusal, Mr. Bones. You see, my job description is very simple. I keep the streets of this city - my city - clean of filth. Unfortunately, that job description allows me very little rest, for the filth never seems to quite scrub off. This city is filled to the brim with scum and lowlifes, dirtying the streets so much that I can barely see the cobble beneath. The guard can barely handle them as is. It would prove great foolishness for me to add your people to this vile mix - simple, brutish barbarians who could snap a man's neck for saying a few wrong words. You do not understand our customs, you are incompatible with our culture, and you will cause nothing but trouble if you walk our streets. However, as retainers, you will be away from interaction with most of the populace, kept at the noble estates. You might not consider such a job honest, but it is a fair livelihood - your workers will serve my nobles, entertain their wishes for a fair income, and stay clear off my streets. I do not need more workers flooding the docks and markets, Thousand Bones. I need servants. If you accept these terms, please appoint another meeting for us next month. Now, if you may - I must prepare for the rest of my day." With that, she went back to her documents, leaving the stunned Shoanti to be escorted outside the building by the guard.  

"Do not tell me you possibly consider her offer, grandfather!" cried Gaekhen when they were back in the street. "Such humiliation for such a proud people... and for what? Your wishful fantasies of co-existing with these... pale devils?" Despite his protests, Thousands Bones stopped the youth with a morose look. "I'm sorry, my child. I think we have no choice. I think we must accept. We mustn't fight back against the arrival of the future - and like it or not, this city is the future. Perhaps, with time, it will grow to accept us as more than servants." Gekhan looked at Kebub for support, but received none. "As much as I hate it, the old man is right. We have to cooperate." The orc grunted. "If our people won't receive their compensation from work, they shall get it from war. And we cannot allow that to happen." Gaekhan, feeling betrayed and devastated, tried to once more rebuff their arguments, but Thousand Bones declared he was tired of the day, and requested to go rest and continue this discussion some other time.  

As they walked behind him to some inn they knew nearby - the Three Rings - Kebub tried to cheer his friend. "I know you must not understand, but there is wisdom in your grandfather's words. He is a fine leader for our people, and wishes nothing but to see them prosperous and happy." "Still, I cannot abide by this decision - and cannot see how you, of all people, would support it." Gaekhen sighed. "But for now we leave it - this evening, we partake in merriment! After all, if there is one thing the devils of this city do well, is brew strong ale!" And so the companions entered the tavern, sent their old shaman to rest, and drank their sorrows away. After finishing his drink, Kebub notices something curious - a Harrow card stuck at the bottom of the mug. It was *The Big Sky*, represented by a group of prisoners tasting their freedom for the first time. It, too, had a similar writing on it's back - *"I know what [[Gaedren Lamm]] has done to you. I know you wish for it to never happen again...*  and the same address at the end. Finishing another drink with Gaekehn, Kebub exclaimed he had business in the city tonight, and left his friend to wallow. Following the directions on the card, he wandered what mystic fate draws him...

Zim whistled to himself, fiddling with his cards as he walked across the piers of [[Korvosa#Old Korvosa|Old Korvosa]]. The day was cloudy and the familiar scents of brinewater churning in the river hit his nose. While crossing a bridge, a wave of wind hit him; Above, a flight of [[Sable Company]] marines, mounted on [[Hippogriff|Hippogriphs]] flew in patrol, the majestic beasts staying in formation at all times. ZIm whistled in awe, then continued on.  

At the end of the old piers he found his destination. Five ships, tied together to a single pier with a mishmash of ropes and plankboards, decorated with colorful paintings and spider-shaped lanterns. This was Eel's End, the crime den of [[Devargo Baravasi]], the King of Spiders, and hopefully, his future employer. Zim approached two scruffy looking men in leather - a Human and a [[Halfling]] - and requested to meet Devargo. "What are ya, some kin' of guardsman? Getoutta here, kid." Barked the halfling, as both men reached for their clubs. Starting to sweat, Zim put on an easy smile and explained he was a friend of Rita. "Why didntya say so before, then? Rita is practically family around here!" the other man smiled, revealing a mouth full of crooked teeth. Zim let his heartbeat rest as he followed the two onto the biggest ship on the pier, an old warship swaying gently on the river.  

The three entered the ship's hall, where Zim finally got to meet the kingpin himself: Devrago was a huge slab of a man, Varisian like himself, with a stern look that could pierce even [[Asmodeus]] himself. His two goons stood next to him, as Zim noticed, somewhat to his horror, small spiderlings crawling over Devargo's face and body. The pests did not seem to bother the man. "Boss, this here lad is Zim, a friend of Rita's from Trails End," exclaimed the Halfling, "he wants to work for us!" Devargo furrowed his brow. "Work? I'm not sure he has the right skills. What can you do, boy?" "Well, I'm pretty good with the cards - I can perform, make some money for you off the streets...." Zim murmured, which Devargo responded to with a dry chuckle. "Clearly you do not understand what sort of job you are applying for. However, I think we can give you a test run right now." He signaled some other goon who appeared at the door, and before long they entered with some crookish frail man wearing a bandanna. "It's done, boss! All of the Shiver supply has been sold on the street." The man grinned. "Incredible job, Borgi. I am very satisfied with your work lately." Before the man had the chance to grin even brighter, Devargo put one massive hand on his shoulder. "However, I also happened to have heard rumors about your conduct with Halvara's girl. Is it true, Borgi? Were you the one who gave her that black eye?" Borgi gulped. "Well, yes, boss, but you see, that annoying bitch was--" before he could finish his sentence, Devargo's hand slipped from his shoulder and grabbed him by the neck, and he started to gag. "Hold him, and keep his mouth open." Devargo coldly commanded his goons. A single spider slowly crept up his arm - a poisonous black widow - and he put the thing in Zim's hands. "Here is your audition, kid. In this family we do not tolerate wanton violence. We watch each other's backs. If you wanna be one of us, you need to show your loyalty. Feed him that spider. Show him what punishments await to those who hurt the people I promised to protect." Taking one good look at the terrified crook, then at the spider, Zim decided the best action would be to... run. He threw the creature at the ground and took a leap towards the door. However, Devargo's men caught him soon enough, dragging him back.  The King of Spiders sighed. "Like I thought. Not ready at all." He let go of Borgi, and let the Black Widow creep back up his arm. "As you heard, I will not tolerate your behavior in my crew. You are hereby banished, Borgi. If I ever see you again near Eel's End, you are a dead man." Borgi, in turn, took his legs and got out of the place quickly.  "As for you, Zim," Devargo continued, sitting back down in his chair, "You are a good lad, but obviously not ready for what we do here. Still - you are kin, and a friend of Rita. You could try to make some earnings for me, and i'll consider letting you join the crew. Go to the streets and do some of those card trick of yours, while my men will nick purses off the audience." Before they left, Devargo said to Zim, "I like you, kid. Don't you disappoint me. Remember I take good care of family."  

The next few hours were passed in the streets of [[Korvosa#Five Corners|Five Corners]]. As Zim performed some "mystical" card readings for the ongoers who stopped to look and admire, Devargo's two goons pickpocketed what they could. When the crowds started to disperse, they took a break, assessing the loot. "Seems your idea wasn't half bad, kid." Said the halfling as he counted coins. "People really seemed to be taken with your shenanigans!" While Zim played with his deck, admiring the old crumply Harrow cards he received from his mentor so many years ago, he noticed something strange. One of the cards had a double... and definitely not his, from it's art style. It was *The Hidden Truth* - the gleaming of something behind the regular and the understood. And behind it - of course - a mysterious message. *"My child. You wish to know who is responsible for your troubles and sorrow. I know, and I can help you bring them to justice. Come to my home at sundown."* Not a stranger to cryptic visions, yet still intrigued by it's meaning, Zim packed his cards, bid farewell to Devargo's men, and went out to find what fate has in store for him...  

--- 

Barton was the first to arrive at the house on Lancet Street, entering the door just as the sun set in the sea at his back. The modest apartment had a small parlor with a kitchen, a bedroom to the side, and some other small room closed off with a colorful drape. The place was warmly lit by candles and a lantern, and the smell of burning incense strongly hit his nose. *Without doubt, Varisians inhabit this home* Barton thought to himself, seeing the Harrow iconography beautifully painted on the walls. On a dining table standing on some colored rugs, he found a basket covered with cloth, containing some crab-meat pies and a bottle of wine, as well as a [[2) Note found in food basket|note]]. With no one around, Barton, still somewhat famished, ate two crab pies by himself - they were a little old and dry, but he couldn't complain - and put the rest in his bag for later.  

A couple of minutes later, and Kebub arrived - eyeing the weird man inside. "Are you the one who summoned me here, Orc?" Barton asked suspiciously. "No. I was called as well." Kebub showed him his card. "I guess that you also have some unfinished business with Lamm." He walked over to the table and, noticing the wine bottle, burst it's head off and drank the entire thing by himself. "I do have some question I need answers to." Barton raised an eyebrow watching the large warrior chugging down the wine. "Pleasure to make your aquaintance. The name is Bob." "I am Kebub, of the Shoanti." Burped the orc. "And you definitely need to take a shower."  

The two sat there in awkward silence until Zim finally entered the house. "I assume you fellows also got this card?" he curiously presented his. Noticing the empty food basket and wine bottle, his lips curled in disappointment. Still, his more talkative nature got the two other men to speak some more, and soon the conversation was flowing, and the three got to know each other a little better - although none of them seemed to truly admit the reason for them being there. During their banter, Barton took a moment to look around. Something was wrong with this house, or rather... too right. The warmness of the light, the dreamy incense in the air. It was all... just slightly too nice, too inviting. "I am starting to wander about the identity of our mysterious patron." He said. "Do you perhaps think it's fate that brought us together tonight?" Offered Zim with a smile. "I don't really believe in such things as fate..." Barton replied, when suddenly, a fourth voice echoed in the room. "Do not be so quick to dismiss fate, friend. It is a powerful force." Pushing aside the curtain from the small room next to them, emerged a Varisian woman in a simple dress. "How long were you standing there? Who are you?" Barton held strongly to his weapon. "Please, you will understand all in time. My name is [[Zellara Esmeranda]]. I am the one who summoned you here. I shall be simple and frank with all of you - I have nothing to hide, and you have already showed much patience by putting up with my... unconventional method of reaching you." She stepped in front of the group. "You see, I brought you here tonight because I believe the three of you have the ability to bring [[Gaedren Lamm]] to justice, once and for all. He and his brutish family has hurt all of you, that I know for sure. He has done awful things to countless people in this city. As for me, he... he took my son. Murdered him in cold blood, after the brave boy tried to stand against his thugs when they took my purse and my Harrow deck, my family heirloom, from me. My kid managed to bring it back home, but he was followed. When he exited the house the next night... they waited for him there."  

She breathed in, and continued. "I sought help from the [[Korvosan Guard]], but they turned me away. Why would they help? They have bigger fish to fry, or richer clientele to protect. So I asked around. I paid bribes. I consulted the cards, and finally I got my answers - I learned that you three could help me, and what's more, I learned where Lamm dwells. He currently squats in an old fishery just north of here, at Westpier 17. But I could not hope to face him on my own, and the Guard moves so slowly he would surly evade them in advance, just like he did for decades now. But, again, I need not tell you about such frustrations. Gaedren has hurt all of you. He has hurt this city - our home - for years now, and will continue to do so until someone steps forth. This is the opportunity to lock him up, or end him for good. And I believe you have the capability to do so. So, there we are. Call it justice, call it cold retribution - it's time for Gaedren Lamm to pay."  

It took not much more convincing from the desperate woman - it seemed all three of the adventurers were in agreement that the city would be better without Gaedren Lamm in it, and that something must be done. Barton had his suspicions about the mysterious Harrow reader and her motives, although Zim seemed excited and ecstatic. "Truly the threads of fate have been woven into a magnificent tapestry tonight! How else could you explain our meeting here?" he smiled. "Well, I'll only believe in this fate you speak of when Lamm is six feet under." Grunted Kebub. As the three organized to hit Gaedren this very night, Zellara had one last request from them. "I wish to do a Harrow reading for each of you. I know some of you do not believe in such mysticism, but for my people, the Harrow is of great spiritual value - I very much believe the reading would help you in your mission, and will let you glimpse some of what is to come."  

Kebub decided to be the first to get his reading, and stepped with Zellara into her Harrow room. Closing the drapes and lighting the incense, she sat before him and spread her cards on the table. "First, we conduct *The Choosing*," she said. "Ask any question you might have, and the cards will guide you." "Do my people have a place in this city?" he asked. Zellara revealed *The Courtesan*. "Master of social niceties, and political intrigue. The courtesan could hint on some future dealings with the nobility or the crown, perhaps?" Kebub frowned, given the suggestion Thousand Bones received earlier that day. Still, they moved on to *The Spread*. "Each column represents the past, present, or future." She explained to him as he chose nine cards from her hand. "Each row is positive, negative, or unclear. Together, the nine shall paint your story for us." He laid his cards in the requested table, and one by one, she started to flip them and explain their meaning.  

![](https://i.postimg.cc/MZP55xKK/kebub-reading1.jpg)

Kebub's cards showed courage, comradeship, and tests to be had, although none were especially strongly aligned. "Keep to your friends, and beware of your entangling past. *The Mountain Man* is a great physical force outside our control - if it's rolling in your present, could you perhaps harness it for your own use?" Zellara suggested.  

Kebub left the booth, thinking this experiment proved very little - what point is there in guessing the past and futures with finicky pictures and cryptic explanations? There is only what was done, and what will be. Still, out of respect to the Varisian, he invested some thought into her words. Next up entered Barton, eager too to disprove the mystical superstitions Zellara and Zim shared. "What is your question for me, child?" Zellara asked as she re-shuffled her cards. "Will I ever be able to clear my name?" he asked her cryptically, yet with honesty. Zellara reveled *The Rabbit Prince*. "An interesting revelation. The Prince represents the skill and capriciousness of combat.... but it can also represent younger members of noble houses. Could that have something to do with your paradigm?" Barton became pale, but quickly decided her offshoot comment was an unlucky guess and nothing but a coincidence, and spread his nine cards on the table.

Once more, Zellara explained each card's meaning carefully and with passion. Cards such as *The Lost*, *The Cyclone*, and *The Big Sky* represented his emptiness, his internal turmoil, his need to belong. With each such reading, Barton's face fell more and more. Finally, she opened *The Peacock*. "Well, *The Peacock* is a card of great beauty, but misaligned there... just means you probably need a good bath and a shave." Zellara said with jest, and though the offshoot insult stung for a moment, the joke did cheer Barton up, distracting him from the sad reading. His mind cleared, he left the booth, focusing once more on the mission at hand.  

Finally, excited Zim entered to receive his reading. "I cannot wait to learn from a master!" he exclaimed, "My old mentor from the carnival used to teach me, but... well, it's hard to find professional Harrow readers in this city!" Zellara smiled. "I'm very glad you continue with this important tradition, my child. What do you wish to reveal?" "I would like to know if what I see would really come true." he asked vaguely. Zellara revealed *The Teamster* with a sly smile. "This little fellow represents an external force that drives us ever on. As I read it, the cards tell us that though they can help us gleam our fate, they cannot help us control it. It is only up to us to determine the future, no matter how we see it. I think you should go with the flow, let life guide you - whatever will happen shall happen." Zim nodded approvingly and spread his cards.  

![](https://i.postimg.cc/XqzgYKDf/zim-reading1.jpg)

This time, Zellara could not hide her alarm - and as she opened more and more cards, her terror. Most of Zim's cards seemed, to her, seemed misfortunes at best and hinting at disaster at worse. *The Lost* and *The Survivor* seemed to tell of some hidden darkness within him, his loneliness in the city a curse he will not soon get rid of, while the strongly aligned *Silent Hag* represents some treacherous secret Zim shall not reveal. "*The Mountain Man* is a returning motif between all three of you, but seems especially menacing here..." she glanced back behind the curtain at the place were Kebub was sharpening his axe, something in the Orc seeming familiar with the giant man. As she opened the final card, representing Zim's negative future, she almost seemed to faint. It was *The Eclipse*. "It represents loss of faith, loss of purpose. Loss of... everything. Honestly... I don't even know what it points at in your future..." Zim did not seem all that terrified as she - he was mainly ecstatic with the mystical revelations, entranced with pictures of the past and the future he almost started to have trouble differentiating. But both regaining their composure, Zim and Zellara joined the other two in the parlor.  

"Well, I do hope this has been enlightening for you. Or entertaining, at the very least." She managed a half smile, attempting not to look at Zim directly. "At any case, I wish you to know this: fate or no, I believe in you with all of my heart. I know I chose the right people for this job. Go now. Go and make our beloved city into just a slightly better place." The three members of the newly found companionship looked at each other, nodded, and left Zellara in her apartment. With the moon now hanging above in the misty sky, they started to make their way northwards, to face the despicable villain who brought them all together. And, perhaps, to face their destiny as well...
